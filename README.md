# Hypothesis Testing Assignment

## Overview
This repository contains solutions to a comprehensive **Hypothesis Testing Assignment**. The objective of the assignment is to deepen understanding of statistical hypothesis testing, including:

- Formulating null and alternative hypotheses
- Selecting significance levels
- Understanding p-values
- Conducting one-sample and two-sample t-tests
- Conducting right-tailed, left-tailed, and two-tailed tests
- Interpreting Type I and Type II errors
- Drawing conclusions from test results

The assignment includes step-by-step solutions to 10 questions, covering both conceptual and practical applications of hypothesis testing.

---

## Assignment Questions

1. **Hypothesis Formulation**  
   Formulate null and alternative hypotheses for a company's claim about an energy drink increasing focus.

2. **Significance Level Selection**  
   Select an appropriate significance level for a study on the effects of exercise on weight loss.

3. **Interpreting p-values**  
   Interpret a p-value of 0.03 in the context of a new teaching method study.

4. **Type I and Type II Errors**  
   Provide scenarios illustrating Type I and Type II errors.

5. **Right-tailed Hypothesis Testing**  
   Test whether a new light bulb lasts more than 1000 hours.

6. **Two-tailed Hypothesis Testing**  
   Determine if there is a difference in mean exam scores between two groups of students.

7. **One-sample t-test**  
   Test whether the mean weight of cereal boxes supports the manufacturer’s claim.

8. **Two-sample t-test**  
   Compare reaction times of two groups in a driving simulation study.

9. **Process Control Example**  
   Evaluate the effect of a new training program on reducing call waiting times.

10. **Interpreting Results**  
    Draw conclusions from a hypothesis test with a p-value of 0.02.

---

## Methodology

For each question:

1. **State Hypotheses**  
   - Clearly define Null (H₀) and Alternative (H₁) hypotheses.

2. **Select Significance Level (α)**  
   - Commonly 0.05 unless specified otherwise.

3. **Choose the Appropriate Test**  
   - Z-test or t-test depending on sample size, population standard deviation, and scenario.

4. **Compute Test Statistic**  
   - Apply formulas for z or t statistics step by step.

5. **Compare with Critical Value or p-value**  
   - Make decisions about rejecting or failing to reject H₀.

6. **Conclusion**  
   - Provide interpretation in plain language.

7. **Type I and Type II Errors**  
   - Discuss potential errors and their implications when applicable.

## Tools & References

- **Formulas Used:**  
  - One-sample t-test: \( t = \frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}} \)  
  - Two-sample t-test: \( t = \frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}} \)  
  - Z-test (for large samples): \( z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} \)
